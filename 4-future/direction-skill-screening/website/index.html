What it screens for

A third-party agent skill (or MCP server) is untrusted code you invite into a privileged session. This tool screens one the way the sibling 3-llm-audit-agent screens a smart contract: treat it as untrusted input, analyse it without granting it real privileges, and attach concrete evidence to every flag so a human can adjudicate it. Three analyzers, all dependency-free and CPU-only:

  • Manifest analysis — diff the declared permissions/verbs against a prior version and flag newly-added sensitive capabilities (privilege creep).
  • Prompt-layer analysis — over the description and docstrings the model reads, detect tool-poisoning and instruction-smuggling, plus the evasion families that beat a naive scanner: homoglyph substitution and encoded (base64/hex) payloads.
  • Behavioural analysis — diff declared capabilities against the egress a sandbox trace actually observed, and flag anything the skill touched but never declared.

Read this honestly

The screening tool reaches 0.967 macro-F1 against the naive baseline's 0.167, and catches 5/6 adversarial skills where the baseline catches 0/6. That gap is the point of the direction: the baseline can only match literal bad words, so a homoglyph, an encoding, or a paraphrase walks straight past it.

But these are numbers on 15 skills the author wrote. A detector graded on its author's own examples will flatter itself; the score is evidence the pipeline runs and the analyzers fire on their target classes, not evidence about real-world skills. The tool also still misses at least one adversarial case (catch rate 0.833, not 1.000) — a zero-width-space evasion the current analyzers do not normalize — and that miss is left in the corpus and reported rather than papered over.

What this skeleton does not establish

  • The corpus is 15 authored skills, not scraped from any real registry. Nothing here generalises to skills in the wild.
  • The proposal's real 500-1000-skill corpus, drawn from live skill/MCP registries with provenance and licensing, is future work under the funded collaboration -- along with hardening the analyzers against evasions the skeleton still misses (e.g. zero-width / character-spacing obfuscation), a provisional patent filing, and an installable package.
  • The scores are on the author's own examples. Treat them as a demonstration that the pipeline runs, not as a measured detection rate.
